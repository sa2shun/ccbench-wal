\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Parallel Logging}
\noindent
\textbf{Parallel Logging Algorithms:}~ Pioneering efforts in parallel logging include protocols such as Aether~\cite{aether} and passive group commit~\cite{wang2014nvm}. These approaches allocate isolated log buffers per core, thereby completely eliminating latch contention during log insertion. 
%
A naive partitioning of logs introduces a trade-off: it complicates the precise reconstruction of global dependencies—the exact commit order of transactions—during recovery. To manage these dependencies efficiently, SiloR \cite{zheng2014silor} introduced an epoch-based mechanism that flushes the log records of all cores at fixed time intervals (i.e., epochs). 
Taurus \cite{xia2021taurus} embeds logical timestamp vectors into log records to capture data dependencies among transactions. By dynamically reconstructing a dependency graph during the recovery phase, Taurus achieves both out-of-order parallel log writes and accurate recovery.
%

\noindent
\textbf{NVRAM aware Logging:}~The emergence of non-volatile RAM (NVRAM) has brought a paradigm shift to the design of parallel logging protocols. %Because NVRAM offers byte-addressability and low latency, it challenges the fundamental premise of traditional Write-Ahead Logging (WAL), which relies on translating in-memory data modifications into a serialized, disk-oriented log stream.
%
For instance, Write-Behind Logging (WBL) \cite{wbl} leverages the persistence of NVRAM by flushing the modified data itself directly to the persistent layer before a transaction commits. By recording only minimal commit metadata in the log, WBL drastically reduces the overhead of generating Redo logs. Other studies, such as Rethinking Logging for NVRAM \cite{rethinking} or similar fine-grained approaches~\cite{IPL}, propose parallel, fine-grained logging on NVRAM.
